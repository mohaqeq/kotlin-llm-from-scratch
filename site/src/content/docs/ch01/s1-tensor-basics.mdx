## What I build

In this section I build `Tensor`, the n-dimensional array that every later chapter uses. When the section is
complete, a tensor has:

- a shape and a size, with checks for wrong data, negative dimensions and `Int` overflow,
- row-major strides, and element access through them: `t[i, j]` and `t[i, j] = v`,
- the factories `Tensor.zeros(...)` and `Tensor.ones(...)`,
- views that share the data: `reshape` and `transpose`,
- `contiguous()`, which copies a view into row-major order,
- `toString()` in nested brackets.

## The idea: storage and view

A tensor has two parts:

1. The **storage**: one flat `DoubleArray` with all values.
2. The **view**: a shape and strides that tell how to read the flat array as n dimensions.

The **stride** of a dimension is the number of positions that one step in that dimension moves in the storage.
The position of an element is the dot product of its index with the strides:

```text
position(i, j, k) = i·strides[0] + j·strides[1] + k·strides[2]

storage  [ 1  2  3  4  5  6 ]          view 1: shape [2, 3], strides [3, 1]    view 2: shape [3, 2], strides [1, 3]
           0  1  2  3  4  5                      1 2 3                                   1 4
                                                 4 5 6                                   2 5
                                                                                         3 6
```

The same six numbers are a 2×3 matrix in view 1 and its transpose in view 2. No value moves. Only the strides change.

| Tensor | Shape | Size | Row-major strides |
|---|---|---|---|
| scalar | `[]` | 1 | `[]` |
| vector | `[5]` | 5 | `[1]` |
| matrix | `[2, 3]` | 6 | `[3, 1]` |
| batch of matrices | `[2, 3, 4]` | 24 | `[12, 4, 1]` |
| empty | `[2, 0, 3]` | 0 | `[0, 3, 1]` |

In the GPT, these views are everywhere. Attention changes `[batch, tokens, 768]` into `[batch, tokens, 12, 64]` with
a reshape, and it computes `Q·Kᵀ` with a transpose. Both are free when they only change the view.
